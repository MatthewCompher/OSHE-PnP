V3 Hardware

V3 is a complete rework of the motion system and toolhead. It keeps the original idea: start from an Ender 3, reuse its extrusion, motors and hardware, and 3D print the rest. A scrap or non-working Ender 3 can save about $100 in parts, but you don't need one. Exact extrusion and belt lengths are not critical, as long as the proportions stay consistent.

Z runs "upside down" on this machine. Z = 0 is homed at the top, and a larger Z moves the nozzle lower. The nozzle touches the staging plate at about Z 50 and the top of a PCB at about Z 38.

Subsystems

Frame

The frame uses the Ender 3's aluminum extrusion with printed legs (bottom, middle and spacer pieces, in A and B mirror versions) and printed 4040 spacers. Parts are in CAD/V3-Active/Framing.

CoreXY motion

Two stepper motors share the X and Y work through a crossed belt path. Each corner has a printed motor mount or idler mount with a belt tensioner (A and B versions). The X-axis uses upper and lower carriage pieces and a rail spacer. Parts are in CAD/V3-Active/Framing/TOP motor A|B, TOP idler A|B and CAD/V3-Active/X-Axis.

Toolhead

The V3 toolhead is based on the LumenPnP design. It uses Samsung CP40 nozzle tips and a NEMA 11 stepper to rotate the nozzle. The rotation axis has no endstop: homing sets the current angle to 0. The down camera rides on the same carriage, about 8 mm (X) and 53 mm (Y) from the nozzle.

Staging plate

The staging plate holds the board and the calibration fixtures. It is built from printed tiles: regular tiles plus one tile with an opening for the up camera. It sits on brackets and spacers. On top are:

  • PCB mounts: one static and one adjustable (dynamic) mount hold the board (8–10 mm tall).
  • Camera datum: a printed 4 mm grid with white dots. The cameras use it as a fixed reference, and datum dot 0 is the visual homing point (11 mm tall). There is a multi-color .3mf version and a "NO AMS" version printed as separate parts.
  • Reject bin: where parts that fail inspection are dropped (15 mm tall).

Parts are in CAD/V3-Active/Staging.

Feeders

V3 uses a modified version of Mark Maker's 0816 low-profile tape feeder, adapted to standard fasteners. Each feeder has a micro servo: one stroke (0° → 90° → 0°, about 2 s) advances the tape by one part. An Arduino Nano runs the servos one at a time so they never draw current together. Parts are in CAD/V3-Active/Feeder (left end, right end, rib).

Pneumatics

V3 uses an air compressor (V2 used a diaphragm vacuum pump) and a solenoid valve switched by the controller board.

⚠ Verify: compressor model, valve, tubing and how vacuum is generated are not documented yet. Pneumatics are still being finished.

Touchscreen (HMI)

A touchscreen on a printed arm (CAD/V3-Active/Electrical/HMI) runs KlipperScreen, so jobs can be started without a laptop. The project moved to a Raspberry Pi 4 for compatibility with this screen.

CAD files

Everything is in CAD/ as STEP (and some STL/3MF). The master file is CAD/V3-Active/PnP_Full_Assembly_V3.step. The models were made in Fusion 360, SolidWorks and Onshape. Ender 3 Full Assembly.step is included as a reference.

3D printing

  • Material: PETG preferred for structural parts and motor mounts; PLA is fine for enclosures and lighter parts.
  • Settings: 0.2 mm layers, 2+ perimeters, 12% gyroid infill, tree supports, 0.4 mm nozzle.
  • Tested on: Bambu Lab A1, Prusa CORE One and Voron 2.4.
